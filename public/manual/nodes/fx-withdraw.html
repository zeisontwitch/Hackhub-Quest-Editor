Charge the player

Effects  fx.withdraw

What it does

Takes money out of the player's bank account. Either a fixed sum, or a slice of whatever they currently have.

When you'd use it

Use it for a purchase, a fine, a bribe, or a tool the player buys. Anything where the story costs the player money.

The percentage mode is what you want for a fee that scales with wealth. A fixed sum is what you want for a price tag.

The player pays around $35 for a tool, so price story purchases somewhere near that.

In the game, this looks like

The player's balance drops, and their bank statement gains a line with the label you typed.

Fields

Amount type

A fixed sum, or a slice of whatever the player currently has in the bank.
What to put here
Pick which of the two numbers below this node reads. <b class="ui">Fixed amount</b> reads <b class="ui">Amount</b>. <b class="ui">Percentage of balance</b> reads <b class="ui">Percent</b>.
Default
"fixed"
Limits
One of the 2 listed below.
Watch out
Both number fields stay visible whichever you pick, and the one you did not pick is ignored. Check this first when a charge comes out wrong.
ChoiceWhat it is stored as
Fixed amountfixed
Percentage of balancepercent

Amount

Credits taken from the player's account.
What to put here
The sum to take, in dollars.
Example
35
Default
100
Limits
0 or more.
If you leave it empty
Nothing happens. The charge is skipped with no warning and no line in the bank statement.

Percent

Percentage of the player's current balance, taken when this node runs.
What to put here
The share of the player's current balance to take. 10 takes a tenth of whatever they have right now.
Default
10
Limits
0 to 100.
Watch out
The result is rounded to a whole number, so a small balance can produce a charge of zero.

Description

The label on the bank statement line.
Example
Tool licence — packet sniffer
Default
"Purchase"
Limits
Technically any text, but keep it short. A statement line is only a few words.
If you leave it empty
The statement line has no label, so the player sees money vanish from nowhere.

A wire only joins two sockets of the same colour, and the editor draws the wire in that colour. That is what the colours are for: match the colours and the connection takes, mismatch them and it will not.

Step by step: add this node to a quest

  1. Find Charge the player under Effects in the palette.
  2. Drag it onto the canvas where the purchase happens.
  3. Pick Amount type before you type a number.
  4. Type the amount, then write a statement label the player will recognise.
  5. Add a Notify after it so the player notices the charge.

Common mistakes

  • The amount is 0 or blank. Nothing happens and nothing warns you. Check Amount before anything else.
  • The wrong Amount type is selected. Both fields sit on screen and only one is read. A 35 in Amount does nothing while Amount type says Percentage of balance.
  • A percentage of a small balance rounds to zero. Ten percent of $4 is $0.40, which rounds to nothing. Use a fixed sum for small amounts.

Messages you might see

  • Unreachable — nothing leads to this node, so the charge never happens
