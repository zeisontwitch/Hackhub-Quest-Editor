Timer

Flow control  flow.timer

What it does

Wait X amount of time until the next node fires. Great for when you want the story to hold for a moment. Unlike a Wait, the time keeps passing while the player is away — a timer set two in-game days out lands two in-game days later even if the player saves and quits.

When you'd use it

For things that happen on the world's own schedule: an article hits the wire, a report gets triaged, a deadline lands — while the player is doing something else.

For world events that all land on one in-game date: set several timers to the same date and they fire together.

Unlike a Wait, the time keeps passing while the player is away. A timer set two in-game days out lands two in-game days later even if the player saves and quits — which is what lets a campaign happen in the world's own time.

In the game, this looks like

The story pauses at the timer (the wires after it do not run yet). When the in-game clock reaches the due time, the timer's “Out” wire runs, as if the story jumped back to this point. If the quest finishes or is abandoned first, the timer is cancelled.

Wires

SocketDirectionWhat to connect
Ininput Whatever should run immediately before this node.
Outoutput Whatever should happen after this node runs.

Then runs next When tests a condition Unlocks opens an objective Data carries a value

A wire only joins two sockets of the same colour, and the editor draws the wire in that colour. That is what the colours are for: match the colours and the connection takes, mismatch them and it will not.

Common mistakes

  • Treating the time as real minutes. The clock is the game's, not the wall clock's. One in-game minute is one real second at default speed, and a full in-game day is about 24 real minutes of play.
  • Expecting the timer to fire after the quest is over. A timer belongs to its quest. Complete or abandon the quest before the due time and the timer is cancelled — nothing arrives afterwards.
  • Setting an in-game date from your own calendar. The in-game clock runs at 60× your wall clock and starts where the save started. Read the year, month, day and time off the in-game clock itself, then work backwards from there.

Messages you might see

  • Nothing scheduled — no time or full date is set, so the timer fires the moment the story reaches it
  • The timer has nothing to do — the “Out” socket is unwired, so the story stops at the timer when it fires

Related nodes

  • Branch — Sends the story down one of two paths.
  • Wait — Pauses the story.
  • Reroute — A tidy point for your wires.
