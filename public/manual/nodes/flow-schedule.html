Schedule beat

Flow control  flow.schedule

What it does

Arms a beat on the in-game clock: the story stops here, and continues down “Out” when the game time you set has passed.

When you'd use it

For story beats that happen on the story's own schedule: an article hits the wire, a report gets triaged, a deadline lands — while the player is doing something else.

Unlike a Wait, the time keeps passing while the player is away. A beat set two in-game days out lands two in-game days later even if the player saves and quits — which is what lets a campaign happen in the world's own time.

In the game, this looks like

The story pauses at the beat (the wires after it do not run yet). When the in-game clock reaches the due time, the beat's “Out” wire runs, as if the story jumped back to this point. If the quest finishes or is abandoned first, the beat is cancelled.

Wires

SocketDirectionWhat to connect
Ininput Whatever should run immediately before this node.
Outoutput Whatever should happen after this node runs.

Then runs next When tests a condition Unlocks opens an objective Data carries a value

A wire only joins two sockets of the same colour, and the editor draws the wire in that colour. That is what the colours are for: match the colours and the connection takes, mismatch them and it will not.

Common mistakes

  • Treating the time as real minutes. The clock is the game's, not the wall clock's. One in-game minute is one real second at default speed, and a full in-game day is about 24 real minutes of play.
  • Expecting the beat to fire after the quest is over. A beat belongs to the quest's story. Complete or abandon the quest before the due time and the beat is cancelled — nothing arrives afterwards.

Messages you might see

  • Nothing scheduled — no days, hours or minutes are set, so the beat fires the moment the story reaches it
  • The beat has nothing to do — the “Out” socket is unwired, so the story stops at the beat when it fires

Related nodes

  • Branch — Sends the story down one of two paths.
  • Wait — Pauses the story.
  • Reroute — A tidy point for your wires.
  • Random pick — Picks one path at random.
  • Sequence — Fires several outputs in order.
  • Debug probe — Prints what is happening here.
