Objective

Objectives  objective

What it does

A task for the player. It appears in their journal, and it ticks over when the condition you wire in comes true.

When you'd use it

Give the player one objective per thing they have to do. The journal is how they know what to do next, and a quest with no objectives reads as broken.

Wire a When event node into the Trigger socket. That is the condition that ticks the objective over.

Keep objectives small. One objective per step gives the player a sense of progress, and gives you somewhere to hang the next beat.

In the game, this looks like

The player opens their journal and reads the line you wrote. When the trigger fires, the line moves to their completed list.

Fields

Identifier

A short unique name for this objective so other nodes can refer to it. Lowercase with dashes reads well.
Example
dock-shipment
Default
empty
If you leave it empty
Other nodes have nothing to refer to. Name the objective before you wire anything to it.

Shown to the player

The line shown in the player's quest journal. A clear instruction tends to read well, but any wording that fits your quest works.
What to put here
Write it as an instruction: what to do, and where.
Example
Find the shipment manifest on the dock terminal.
Default
empty
Limits
Technically any text, but keep it to one line. The journal truncates a long one.
If you leave it empty
The journal shows a blank task. The player has no idea what they are meant to do.

Hint

Revealed only if the player asks for help. As gentle or as cryptic as you want it to be.
Example
The manifest sits on the machine by the gate, not the office.

Extra info

Extra detail in the journal's expanded view. Good for lore or background.
What to put here
Lore belongs here rather than in the journal line.
Example
Meridian Capital moved the shipment twice after the strike.

Suggested command

Suggested command shown in the journal. It runs nothing — it is a copy-pasteable nudge.
Example
ssh dock01.meridian.local
Watch out
It runs nothing on its own. It is a nudge the player can copy, not a shortcut.

Hidden until unlocked

Keep the objective out of the journal until another objective unlocks it. Use for twists.
Default
off
Limits
On or off.

Wires

SocketDirectionWhat to connect
Ininput Whatever should run immediately before the objective is handed to the player — usually Quest start or the objective before it.
Triggerinput The When event node that ticks this objective over.
Unlocked byinput An objective that has to finish before this one becomes available.
On completeoutput Whatever should happen once the player finishes — the next objective, a payment, the ending.
Unlocksoutput The objective this one opens up.

Then runs next When tests a condition Unlocks opens an objective Data carries a value

A wire only joins two sockets of the same colour, and the editor draws the wire in that colour. That is what the colours are for: match the colours and the connection takes, mismatch them and it will not.

Step by step: add this node to a quest

  1. Find Objective under Objectives in the palette.
  2. Drag it onto the canvas.
  3. Wire something into its In socket, so the player actually receives it.
  4. Add a When event node and wire its When socket into Trigger.
  5. Write the journal line in Shown to the player.
  6. Wire the next beat of the story to On complete.

Common mistakes

  • Nothing is wired to Trigger. The objective sits in the journal forever. The player can do everything right and it never ticks over.
  • Nothing is wired to In. The player never receives the objective at all, so the journal stays empty.
  • The trigger listens for the wrong event. The player does the thing and nothing happens. Check the event list for the one your action really raises.

Messages you might see

  • No trigger — nothing is wired to Trigger, so the player can never finish this objective
  • Unreachable — nothing leads to this node, so the player never receives the objective
